About Flapjack Recipe

By Michael

Flapjack Recipe is a small British website about one bake: the flapjack. One dish, done properly — researched carefully, written out in full, and labelled honestly.

Who runs this website?

My name is Michael. I am a home cook in the UK, and I also run Cottage Pie Recipe, a sister site built on the same idea: take one much-loved British dish, research it thoroughly, and publish the clearest, most honest version of the recipe I can — with the variations, the troubleshooting and the questions people actually ask, all in UK English with grams, millilitres, °C, fan and Gas Mark.

Why a whole website about flapjacks?

Because the flapjack looks simple and still goes wrong constantly. Search for the recipe and you will find oven temperatures anywhere from 150°C to 200°C and times from 15 to 40 minutes, with no explanation of why. The result is a lot of hard, crumbly or greasy bars and a lot of confused bakers. This site exists to fix that: one classic recipe as the anchor, a set of variations that each change one thing at a time, and guides that explain the reasons — why boiling the syrup makes bars hard, why pressing matters, why the centre should still look soft when the tin comes out.

How recipes are tested — and labelled

Every recipe here begins as careful desk work: published recipes from trusted sources are compared, the ratios are cross-checked, and a single clear version is written out. A recipe is only described as kitchen-tested once it has actually been cooked, exactly as written, and confirmed to work. Until then it carries a plain note saying it is a draft. That distinction matters to me, and it will never be blurred on this site.

Our honesty rules

Photographs on this site are real photographs, honestly captioned: a caption says what the photo actually shows, and where a photograph is used under a free licence, the photographer is credited, as the licence asks. No photograph is ever described as something it is not. We do not publish ratings or reviews we do not have, nutrition figures we have not calculated from a named source, or claims we cannot stand behind. Where something is an estimate, it is marked as an estimate.
