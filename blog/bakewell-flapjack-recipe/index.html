Bakewell Flapjack Recipe

By Michael

A Bakewell flapjack takes the two flavours that define a Bakewell tart — almond and raspberry — and bakes them into a chewy oat bar instead of a pastry case. Ground almonds and a little almond extract go through the mixture, raspberry jam is swirled over the top before baking, and flaked almonds scatter over everything to toast in the oven. There is no pastry to make, no frangipane to mix and no icing to pipe: just the Bakewell taste in a tin of flapjacks, cut into bars for lunchboxes, bake sales and afternoon tea.

The short answer: Melt 175g butter, 175g golden syrup and 175g light muscovado sugar together. Stir in 300g porridge oats, 50g ground almonds, ½ teaspoon of almond extract and a pinch of salt. Press into a lined 20cm square tin, dot 3 tablespoons of raspberry jam over the top and swirl it lightly through the surface, then scatter over 25g flaked almonds. Bake at 160°C (140°C fan, Gas Mark 3) for 30–35 minutes. Cool completely in the tin, then cut into 12 bars.

Kitchen-tested: Michael has cooked this recipe exactly as written in his own kitchen and confirmed it works. Times are a guide — use the cues in each step.

Bakewell flavours in a flapjack: what carries over, and what does not

A traditional Bakewell tart is built in layers: a shortcrust pastry case, a spread of raspberry jam on the base, an almond frangipane sponge over the top, and then either a scattering of flaked almonds or a coat of white icing with a glacé cherry. It is a lovely thing, but it is also a project — pastry to roll, a case to line, a sponge to mix, and a tart that needs eating fairly promptly.

The flapjack version keeps the two flavours that do the real work — almond and raspberry — and lets the rest go. Ground almonds replace part of the oats, almond extract gives the clear, sweet almond note that frangipane gets from its sponge, and the jam moves from a hidden layer to a swirl across the top, where you can see it. What you give up is the contrast of crisp pastry and soft sponge. What you gain is a bar that keeps for days in a tin, travels without falling apart, and takes about fifteen minutes of actual work. It is Bakewell in flavour, flapjack in everything else — and this page will call it that, rather than pretend it is a tart.

The starting point is our tested classic flapjack: the butter, syrup and sugar quantities, the tin and the oven temperature are all unchanged. Only the dry ingredients shift, and the jam and flaked almonds are additions on top. That matters, because the binding of a flapjack lives in the melted butter and syrup coating every oat — and that part of the recipe is exactly as tested.

You also need a 20cm square tin and a sheet of baking parchment to line it. Everything else — a saucepan, a spoon, a knife for swirling — is standard kitchen kit.

What the ground almonds do: a softer, richer bar

Swapping 50g of the oats for ground almonds changes the texture of the finished bar in two ways. First, ground almonds bring their own fat, so the bar tastes richer and more buttery than the classic, even though the butter quantity has not changed. Second, they soften the set slightly: a ground almond is a fine, oily powder rather than a chewy flake, so a bar with ground almonds in it cuts a little more tender and crumbles a little more readily at the edges.

That is why the swap stops at 50g. The oats are not just filling — their flakes are the structure that the syrup glues together. Push the ground almonds much beyond a seventh of the dry weight and the bars start to lose their chew and become fragile. Fifty grams is enough to taste the almond clearly, especially with the extract behind it, while the 300g of oats still does the structural work. If you want a stronger almond hit, add another quarter teaspoon of extract rather than more ground almonds.

The method, step by step

  1. Heat the oven and line the tin. Set the oven to 160°C (140°C fan, Gas Mark 3). Line a 20cm square tin with baking parchment, leaving a little overhang at two sides so the slab lifts out easily later.
  2. Melt the butter, syrup and sugar. Put the butter, golden syrup and light muscovado in a large saucepan on a low heat on the hob. Let everything melt together, stirring now and then, until the sugar has dissolved and the mixture is smooth and glossy. Do not let it boil — boiled syrup sets hard and makes hard flapjacks.
  3. Add the almond extract. Take the pan off the heat and stir in the ½ teaspoon of almond extract. It smells strong at this point; it will mellow in the oven. Off the heat, so the flavour does not cook away before the oats even go in.
  4. Stir in the oats and ground almonds. Tip in the porridge oats, ground almonds and the pinch of salt. Stir until every oat is coated and shiny and there are no dry patches hiding at the bottom of the pan — dry oats mean crumbly bars.
  5. Press the mixture into the tin. Spoon the mixture into the lined tin and press it down firmly and evenly with the back of the spoon, pushing it well into the corners. A level, firmly pressed surface is what lets the jam swirl sit on top instead of sinking in.
  6. Dot and swirl the jam. Drop teaspoonfuls of the raspberry jam across the surface — nine or twelve small dollops, spread out. Drag the tip of a table knife through them once or twice to make loose swirls. Keep the swirling shallow and light: you want ribbons of jam through the top, not jam mixed through the whole bar.
  7. Scatter the flaked almonds. Sprinkle the flaked almonds evenly over the top and press them down very lightly with your palm so they stick to the jammy surface rather than falling off when the bars are cut.
  8. Bake for 30–35 minutes. Bake until the edges are set and golden and the flaked almonds are pale gold. The centre will still look soft and the jam will still be glossy and loose — both firm up as the slab cools. If the almonds are browning fast, your oven is running hot; the bars are probably nearly done.
  9. Cool completely, then cut. Leave the slab to cool in the tin until it is completely cold. Warm flapjack crumbles under the knife, and warm jam smears. Once cold, lift the slab out on the parchment and cut it into 12 bars with a large sharp knife.

The jam swirl: on top, not stirred through

It is tempting to stir the jam through the mixture in the pan, and it works after a fashion — but the jam disappears into the oats and sweetens the whole batch unevenly, and you lose the thing that makes a Bakewell a Bakewell: distinct ribbons of raspberry against almond. Swirled on top, the jam stays where the tart puts it, between you and the almond mixture, and every bar gets a sticky, jammy stripe across its face.

Two practical points. First, small dollops swirl better than three big ones — big spoonfuls sink and pool. Second, the jam will still be soft when the tin comes out of the oven. Raspberry jam does not set in the heat; it sets as it cools, which is one more reason the slab needs to be fully cold before cutting. If you love the raspberry idea but want the fruit in the bar rather than on it, our cherry flapjack recipe takes the opposite approach, with glacé cherries folded right through the mixture.

When it goes wrong: dark almonds and a soft middle

The flaked almonds are the first place trouble shows. They sit on top of the bake, exposed to the oven's full heat, and they can go from pale gold to too dark while the bars underneath are still finishing. Check the tin from about 25 minutes: the almonds should finish pale gold with darker edges, no more. If your oven has a hot spot, turn the tin round halfway through the bake so one side does not take all the heat.

The other common complaint is a middle that feels too soft at cutting time. Ground almonds genuinely do soften this recipe's set compared with the classic, and the swirled jam adds a soft stripe on top — so a bar that feels fragile warm is usually fine once cold. Always judge the set when the slab is completely cold, and always cut cold with a confident, single press of the knife rather than a sawing motion, which tears the jammy top.

Storing Bakewell flapjacks: a tin, and about five days

Once the bars are cut, keep them in an airtight tin at room temperature, with a sheet of baking parchment between layers if you stack them — the jammy tops will stick to the bar above otherwise. They should keep well for up to 5 days, and like most flapjacks they are arguably better on day two, when the flavours have settled and the texture has firmed. There is no need to refrigerate them; the fridge dries flapjacks out and dulls the almond flavour.

To freeze, wrap the bars individually or layer them in a box with parchment between, and freeze for up to 3 months. Defrost at room temperature for a couple of hours before eating. The jam swirl freezes perfectly well; the flaked almonds lose a little of their toastiness but are still good.
