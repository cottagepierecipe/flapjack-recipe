No-Bake Flapjack Recipe

By Michael

A no-bake flapjack swaps the oven for the fridge: butter, peanut butter and honey are melted on the hob, stirred through porridge oats, pressed very firmly into a tin and chilled until set. The bars are softer than a baked flapjack and they live in the fridge, but the method is short, the washing-up is one pan, and there is no oven to heat on a warm day.

The short answer: Melt 125g butter, 125g smooth peanut butter and 100g honey or golden syrup, stir in 300g porridge oats and a pinch of salt, press very firmly into a lined 20cm square tin and chill for at least 2 hours (estimate) until firm. Cut with a hot knife into 12 bars and keep in the fridge for about 5 days (estimate).

Not yet kitchen-tested: this recipe adapts our tested classic flapjack and has not been cooked by us yet. Michael tests every new recipe himself, and this page will be updated once he has cooked it. Times marked (estimate) are estimates.

No-Bake Flapjack Ingredients: Peanut Butter Does the Setting

There is no flour, no egg and no baking powder in a flapjack, baked or otherwise. A baked flapjack sets because the melted butter and syrup cool and firm around the oats; a no-bake flapjack relies on the same trick, with peanut butter added as a second setting fat. Peanut butter is thick at fridge temperature, so it holds the oats together far better than butter alone, which is why a plain butter-and-syrup mixture chilled in a tin tends to crumble at the corners. The honey or golden syrup sweetens and adds a little stickiness while warm, then firms as it cools.

The Method, Step by Step: Melt, Stir, Press, Chill

  1. Line the tin first. Line a 20cm square tin with baking paper, leaving an overhang on two sides so the set slab lifts out in one piece. Do this before anything melts — the mixture waits for no one once it is off the hob.
  2. Melt the fats and honey gently. Put the butter, peanut butter and honey or golden syrup in a large pan over a low heat. Stir as it melts and take it off the heat the moment everything is liquid and combined. It should never boil: boiling drives off water, and a drier mixture sets crumbly rather than firm.
  3. Stir in the oats and salt. Tip in the porridge oats and the pinch of salt and stir until every oat is coated and there are no dry, pale patches at the bottom of the pan. This takes a full minute of stirring; dry oats are the main reason no-bake bars fall apart later.
  4. Press the mixture down very firmly. Scrape everything into the lined tin and press it flat with the back of a spoon, then go over it again with the base of a glass or a flat-bottomed mug, pushing into the corners and edges. Pressing is the step that does the work the oven does in our classic baked flapjack — loose mixture means loose bars, however long you chill them.
  5. Chill for at least 2 hours (estimate). Put the tin in the fridge for at least 2 hours (estimate), and overnight if you can. The butter and peanut butter need to be cold all the way through to the centre before the slab will cut cleanly.
  6. Lift out and cut with a hot knife. Lift the slab out by the paper overhang. Run a large knife under the hot tap, dry it, and cut the slab into 12 bars, wiping and re-warming the knife if it starts to drag. A hot knife slides through the firm fat instead of cracking it.
  7. Store in the fridge. Put the bars in an airtight container with baking paper between the layers and keep them in the fridge.

Why Pressing and Chilling Replace the Oven in a No-Bake Flapjack

In the classic baked flapjack, the oven does three jobs. It melts the butter and syrup into every oat, it dries the surface slightly so the bars feel set, and it drives the sugars to a firmer set as the tin cools. A no-bake version has to cover all three jobs without heat in a tin. Melting on the hob covers the first. Pressing covers the second and third together: packing the oats tightly leaves no air gaps for the bars to break along, and the fridge firms the butter and peanut butter until they act like a glue.

That is also why the texture is honestly different, and it is better to say so than to pretend otherwise. A no-bake flapjack is softer, denser and more yielding than a baked one — closer to a fridge bar than to a chewy baked square. Warm it up in a lunchbox on a hot day and it will slump. If you want the firm, chewy bite of the original, the classic recipe is the one to make; this version is for when the oven is off, the kitchen is already hot, or you want small hands to help with a recipe where nothing goes near a hot oven tray.

Which Oats Set Best in a No-Bake Flapjack: Porridge Oats, Not Jumbo

Porridge oats are rolled thinner than jumbo oats, so they pack down tightly and absorb the melted fat quickly. In a baked flapjack that difference is a matter of taste; in a no-bake flapjack it decides whether the bars hold together at all. Jumbo oats keep their shape and leave gaps between flakes, and without the oven's drying heat the mixture around them never quite knits. If jumbo oats are all you have, pulse half of them briefly in a food processor to break them down, then mix them with the rest — the finer pieces fill the gaps.

Instant oat sachets are not a good swap. They are often pre-sweetened, sometimes flavoured, and milled so fine that the bars set pasty rather than chewy.

Dairy-Free No-Bake Flapjacks: The Coconut Oil Swap

Swap the butter for the same weight of coconut oil and the recipe becomes dairy-free. Coconut oil sets firmer in the fridge than butter, so the bars hold their edges a little better — the trade-off is a faint coconut flavour, which suits the peanut butter well. Use refined coconut oil if you want no coconut flavour at all. Our vegan flapjack recipe uses the same swap in a baked bar, and the setting behaviour described there applies here too: coconut oil melts at a lower temperature than butter, so these bars soften faster out of the fridge than the butter version.

The Peanut Allergy Note: Read This Before You Start

This recipe contains peanuts twice over — the peanut butter itself, and the fact that many porridge oats are packed in factories that handle nuts, so the packet is worth checking too. If anyone eating these bars has a peanut allergy, do not serve this version. The nut-free route is to swap the peanut butter for a seed butter such as sunflower seed butter, weight for weight, and to check every packet for nut warnings; that version is written up in full as our nut-free flapjack recipe, including the ingredient-checking routine. Honey, whichever version you make, must never be given to a baby under 12 months.

How Long No-Bake Flapjacks Keep: About 5 Days in the Fridge (Estimate)

Keep the cut bars in an airtight container in the fridge for about 5 days (estimate). They do not keep at room temperature the way baked flapjacks do, because the set depends on cold fat; a bar left in a warm kitchen softens within the hour. For lunchboxes, pack the bar straight from the fridge and it will be fine to eat at lunchtime in a cool bag, but expect it to be soft by afternoon in summer. You can freeze the bars for up to a month (estimate): wrap them individually, freeze, and thaw in the fridge overnight or for about an hour at room temperature before eating.

Simple Additions That Suit a No-Bake Flapjack

Because nothing is baked, additions that would dry out in an oven work well here. A handful of raisins or chopped dried apricots stirred in with the oats adds sweetness without more syrup. A tablespoon of cocoa powder turns the mixture chocolatey — add it to the melted pan so it dissolves rather than dusting the oats. Desiccated coconut, up to 50g, can replace the same weight of oats. Press a few chocolate chips over the top while the surface is still warm if you like; they will sit on the surface rather than melt into it.
