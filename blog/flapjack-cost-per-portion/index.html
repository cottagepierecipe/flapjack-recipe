How Much Does a Homemade Flapjack Cost Per Portion?

By Michael

Homemade is supposed to be the cheap option, but nobody ever shows the working. This post does. We priced the exact ingredients of our classic flapjack at Tesco, via trolley.co.uk, on 17 November 2026 — the prices are listed below with the date on them, because prices change and a costing without a date is just a guess wearing a suit. From those four prices we work out what one batch costs, what one bar costs, and what is left in the basket afterwards. The answer, up front: about £3.22 a batch, about 27p a bar, from a £7.05 basket.

The short answer: One batch of our classic flapjack — 175g butter, 175g golden syrup, 175g sugar, 350g porridge oats, a pinch of salt — costs about £3.22 in ingredients at the Tesco prices checked on 17 November 2026, which is about 27p per bar across the 12 bars it makes. The whole shopping basket, one pack of each ingredient, costs £7.05, and most of every pack is left over for further batches. That is ingredients only: the oven's energy is not counted, and no shop-bought bar is priced for comparison, because we did not collect a verified like-for-like price for one.

The basket: four prices, checked on the day

One honest substitution to note: our classic recipe is written with light muscovado sugar, and for this costing we priced standard light brown soft sugar, the everyday equivalent most people would actually buy. The oats, butter and syrup are costed exactly as the recipe uses them.

That is the most you can spend on this recipe in one shop, and it is a misleadingly large number for a single batch, because a batch uses only part of every pack. The honest figure for the bake itself is the next table: what the batch actually consumes, priced pro rata.

The working: what one batch costs

The method is the same one used for any honest costing: for each ingredient, take the fraction of the pack the recipe uses and multiply by the pack price. The classic batch uses 175g of butter from a 250g block, 175g of syrup from a 454g tin, 175g of sugar from a 500g bag and 350g of oats from a 1kg bag, plus a pinch of salt allowed at a penny.

So: about £3.22 of ingredients goes into the tin, and twelve bars come out, at about 27p a bar. Every number in that sentence can be re-derived from the two tables above and the prices as they stood on the check date — which is the standard a costing should be held to. Where the money sits inside the batch is less obvious than the total, and it is worth a look, because it is not where most people expect.

Butter: over two-fifths of the cost

The butter is the expensive line, and it is not close. At £1.37, it accounts for over two-fifths of the batch's £3.22 — more than the syrup and the sugar's reputations would suggest, and more than the oats manage at any weight. Butter is simply a costly ingredient per gram: £0.78 per 100g against the oats' £0.14. It is also the ingredient doing the most work in the bar — flavour, richness, and a good part of the set — so the money is not wasted, but anyone costing a bake sale batch or a crowd's worth should look at the butter first. Two batches of flapjack need two blocks' worth of baking, near enough; the butter is where the budget goes.

Syrup and sugar: the sweet middle

The two sweet ingredients cost almost exactly the same as each other — 67p of syrup, 70p of sugar — and between them they match the butter almost to the penny: £1.37 of sweetness against £1.37 of butter. The syrup looks expensive on the shelf because a 454g tin is a considered purchase, but the recipe uses barely over a third of it, so its share of the batch stays modest; the tin then sits in the cupboard, keeping perfectly, for the next batches. The sugar's position is the mirror image: a £2 bag sounds like the dear item in the basket, and per 100g it is mid-table, but 175g of it is only 70p of baking. Shelf price and batch cost are different numbers, and this pair is the proof.

Oats: the main ingredient costs the least

And then the oats, the ingredient the flapjack is actually made of: 350g of them, the largest weight in the recipe by far, and the smallest cost in the batch at 47p. At £1.35 for a whole kilo — £0.14 per 100g — porridge oats are one of the cheapest substantial foods in the shop, and a flapjack is mostly oats wearing a butter-and-syrup coat. This is the arithmetic that makes homemade flapjack such good value in the first place: the bulk of the bar costs almost nothing, and the money goes on the ingredients that flavour and bind it. It is also why cutting the oats to save money would be exactly backwards — you would save pennies and lose the bar.

After the first batch: what the basket does next

The £7.05 basket is not a per-batch cost, because the first batch leaves most of it behind. The packs cover, roughly, two batches' worth of butter, two of syrup and two of sugar, and nearly three batches of oats — so the second batch from the same basket uses another £3.22 worth of ingredients but asks for nothing new at the till until the packs start running out. Put the two views together and the honest summary is: £7.05 to set yourself up, about £3.22 of ingredients per batch after that, about 27p a bar either way. For a bake sale or a crowd, the same arithmetic scales directly — two batches are about £6.44 of ingredients, made from a basket you have probably already bought. Our post on bake-sale flapjacks covers the planning and wrapping side of baking in quantity.

What the 27p does not include

Three honest caveats. First, this is an ingredients costing: the gas or electricity for 30 to 35 minutes in the oven, the baking paper and the washing-up are real costs of a batch, but they are not priced here, because we did not measure them and this post does not invent figures. Second, the prices are a snapshot: they were Tesco's prices, read via trolley.co.uk, on 17 November 2026. Prices move, offers come and go, and another supermarket on another day will give a somewhat different total — the tables above let you re-run the working with whatever prices are in front of you. Third, there is no shop-bought comparison in this post. It would be easy to write "a bought bar costs X, so you save Y" — but we did not collect a verified like-for-like price for a bought flapjack bar, so the claim is not made. If you want that comparison, the fair way is per 100g, from the label of the bar you actually buy, against the per-bar figures here and the per-100g figures on our flapjack nutrition page.

Making each bar cheaper, honestly

The one lever that always works is the knife. The batch costs what it costs — £3.22 — but cut into 16 pieces instead of 12, each portion is about 20p of ingredients. Nothing about the recipe changes; the portions are simply smaller, and for a rich bar that is often the right call anyway. Beyond that, the savings live in not wasting the basket: syrup and oats keep for months, butter freezes, and a second batch planned while the packs are open costs the same £3.22 in ingredients with no new shopping at all. What we would not advise is cheapening the butter or halving the syrup to chase pennies off the 27p — the arithmetic in this post shows the bar is already mostly 47p worth of oats, and the flavour lives in the lines that cost the money.
